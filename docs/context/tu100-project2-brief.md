- About 10 students per group, 6 groups in the section.

## Tools the slides teach (use them in the presentation)

### Problem statement

"*[User]* needs to *[verb: what they need to do or feel]* because *[insight: why it matters]*." Focus on the problem, not the solution.

### Iceberg model (problem analysis)

Look below the surface event: **event** → **patterns / trends** → **systemic structures** (habits, how things are set up) → **mental models** (beliefs behind it).

### Cause-and-effect analysis

Explain the problem, list its causes, list its effects (fishbone / tree style: several causes feed one problem, which leads to several effects).

### Stakeholder analysis

Identify stakeholders before the project starts, group them by participation, interest and influence, and plan how to involve each.

- Related to the problem: cause it (directly/indirectly, fully/partly) or are affected by it (directly/indirectly).
- Related to the solution: major or minor role; direct or indirect beneficiary; allies with similar goals.

Table format used in the slides:

| Stakeholder | Relationship to the problem (interest) | Effect | Importance (high / medium / low) |
|---|---|---|---|

Then plot them on an **interest vs importance** grid.

### Solution strategy

- From causes to **activities**, each with a **goal** and a **KPI** (the sample project ties activities → goals → KPIs).
- **SWOT** per solution idea: strengths and weaknesses (our skills and resources), opportunities and threats (outside our control).

### SMART objectives

Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound, written as one sentence (not one goal per letter). Example pattern: "To work with *[who]* in *[doing what]* by *[date]*", with a KPI such as "work with 20 people, build X, cost Y baht, on date Z". Strategies under a goal also get their own KPIs.

### What makes a successful project (questions to answer)

- What causes and effects does it address?
- Who are the stakeholders?
- What is the proposed solution?
- Which groups might oppose it, and why? How does the project respond to them?
- What are the benefits?

## Project-based learning elements the course grades against

Challenging real problem; learners involved in every step; real-world content matching a community need; learner decisions; reflection; constructive feedback; **public presentation**.
