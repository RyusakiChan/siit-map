## Slide 1. Project topic

**SIIT Map: finding your way in the SIIT Main Building**

- A free 3D map of the 5-floor SIIT Main Building that opens on any phone, no app install.
- Shows where departments, rooms and facilities (restrooms, stairs, elevators, cafe, library) are, in English and Thai.
- Search by room name, number or department.
- "You are here" QR codes at stair and elevator lobbies: scan one and the map shows where you stand and which way you face.
- Type: TU Project (on campus).

## Slide 2. Problem analysis

**Problem statement** (course format):
> Visitors and new students *need to* find rooms, offices and facilities in the Main Building quickly, *because* there is no public map or room list and getting lost costs them time and stress.

Background:
- Nothing room-level is published online: we checked on 29 Oct 2026 and found no floor plans, room lists, room-number scheme or department-by-floor list.
- The building has 5 floors; inside, the only maps are the fire-exit evacuation maps.
- [Survey photos: signage at entrances, lobbies, corridors.]
- [Interview quotes: staff or students who give or ask for directions.]

Why it matters:
- [Who gets lost most: new students, visitors, exchange students who don't read Thai, parents on open days.]
- Ties to civic qualities: self-dependence (people can find their own way instead of asking), respect for equality (equal access for Thai and non-Thai speakers).

## Slide 3. Cause-and-effect analysis

**Causes**
1. No public floor plan or room directory online.
2. Room numbers do not obviously tell you the floor or wing [check on the walk-through].
3. Signage is limited, mostly in one language [check in the survey].
4. Information lives in people's heads: you have to ask staff or friends.

**Problem:** people can't find rooms, offices and facilities in the Main Building.

**Effects**
1. Late for classes, exams and appointments.
2. Staff and guards interrupted to give directions.
3. Visitors and non-Thai speakers feel unwelcome.
4. Facilities (restrooms, elevators for those who need them) are hard to find.

Iceberg view:
- Event: someone is lost in a corridor.
- Pattern: it happens every semester start and every open day.
- Structure: no shared, maintained source of room information.
- Mental model: "ask someone" is seen as good enough, so nobody writes it down.

## Slide 5. Proposed solution and action plan

**Solution:** the SIIT Map web app plus printed "you are here" QR codes.

Options considered (short SWOT):
| Idea | Strength | Weakness | Opportunity | Threat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Web map (chosen) | Free, on every phone, bilingual | Needs accurate data | Can grow to other SIIT buildings | Rooms change; data goes stale |
| Printed directory posters | Simple | Fixed place, one language at a time | Cheap to print | Easy to remove or outdate |
| Ask for more official signs | Permanent | Out of our control, slow | n/a | No budget |
